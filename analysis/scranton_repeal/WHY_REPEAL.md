# Why Scranton dropped its split rate

Research memo, 2026-10-24. Scranton taxed land at about 4.7 times the building rate from 1913
through 2025. For 2026 it levies one City rate on land and buildings alike. This memo collects
what the public record says about why, what it shows about interests the change served, and what
it cannot yet show. Section 3 separates documented facts from hypotheses; nothing here asserts a
motive the record does not state.

Numbers between `AUTOGEN` markers are generated by `why_repeal_old_roll.py` from the cached
2025 County roll. Do not edit them by hand; rerun the script (`--check` reports staleness).

## Summary

- **The only stated reasons are three sentences in the 2026 budget**: most vacant, developable
  land in the city is now built on; Scranton is one of under a dozen of Pennsylvania's 2,560
  municipalities with a split rate; and one rate is simpler to calculate and easier to compare
  with the school district's and county's. No official is on record explaining it further.
  Council adopted the rate 5-0 with no debate. The press covered it as a footnote to the
  countywide reassessment, and no land-tax advocate responded publicly.
- **The change came from the administration, folded into the reassessment year.** It first
  appears in a 2025-10-20 City press release and the 2025-10-31 proposed budget from the
  Business Administrator's office, not in any council motion.
- **Under the old roll, land paid most of the City tax.** On the 2025 roll, land was
  <!--AUTOGEN:LAND_SHARE_AV-->24%<!--/AUTOGEN:LAND_SHARE_AV--> of taxable value but carried
  <!--AUTOGEN:LAND_SHARE_TAX-->59%<!--/AUTOGEN:LAND_SHARE_TAX--> of the levy. Keeping a 4.7:1
  ratio on new, market-value land assessments would have moved bills a second time, on top of
  the reassessment, in a year when the mayor was running for Congress.
- **No record found ties an official's or donor's money to the repeal.** The council president
  owns about ten rental parcels, but on the old roll the repeal barely moves his total. The
  biggest beneficiaries are land-heavy holders: utility land, land-only commercial parcels and
  downtown property, led by the owner of the Marketplace at Steamtown. None was found lobbying for
  the change. The one donor controversy of those weeks, the Fidelity Bank board's contributions
  to the mayor, concerned a building purchase, not the tax rate.
- **The records that could settle the money question are not online.** Those are the
  officials' Statements of Financial Interests, the itemized municipal campaign reports, and
  any internal analysis of the switch. Requests are drafted in
  `WHY_REPEAL_RECORDS_REQUESTS.md`, unsent.

## 1. What officials said

**The 2026 budget narrative** is the whole of the stated rationale. The proposed budget
(2025-10-31, `sources/scranton_budget_2026.pdf`) and the final posted budget (File of the
Council No. 102 of 2025; URL under Sources, local copy git-ignored at 22 MB) carry the same
paragraph. It says:

- the City has levied a split rate since 1913, to encourage development of vacant property;
- over the past century the City has developed most of its vacant, developable property;
- it is one of under a dozen PA municipalities still levying a split rate;
- one rate provides a simpler calculation and a clearer comparison with the Scranton School
  District and Lackawanna County.

Nothing else on the record adds to this:

- **The City press release of 2025-10-20** (`sources/why_repeal/20251020_city_release_no_tax_increase_2026.pdf`)
  mentions the change in one sentence, without a reason. The mayor's and Council President
  Smurl's quotes are about holding taxes flat.
- **The budget presentation to Council (about 2025-11-13)** presented the single millage as
  contingent on the reassessment. Business Administrator Eileen Cipriani was among the
  presenters. Only a paywalled summary was reachable (Citizen Portal). The ECTV video would
  show whether anyone explained the change or asked about it.
- **Council minutes for 2025-11-25 to 2025-12-16** (`sources/`) show the millage ordinance
  introduced 12-02, read 12-09 and adopted 12-16 by roll call: King, Schuster, Rothchild,
  McAndrew and Smurl, all yes. No member spoke on it.
- **The Times-Tribune (Jim Lockwood, 2026-02-07, via Yahoo)** reported that the reassessment
  "gave the opportunity" to end the split. It quotes no one on why. The article also says the
  school district had split rates, which the County millage sheets contradict.
- **The Pennsylvania Economy League's five-year projection (2026-08,
  `sources/why_repeal/pel_five_year_projection_2026-08.pdf`)** describes the switch without
  evaluating it.
- **The City's 2026-10-20 answers to Council** explain a 4% fall in real estate revenue by the
  revenue-neutral first-year rate and installment payments, not the switch.

The Act 47 record says nothing on the split rate either way. The 2017 exit plan, the 2021
termination recommendation (both PEL) and a 2014 HJA Strategies report all push for countywide
reassessment because the 1968 values penalized new construction (`sources/why_repeal/`).

## 2. How the decision was made

| Date | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2025-09-02 | Cognetti announces a run for PA-8 | Wikipedia, per press |
| 2025-10-20 | City release: no tax increase; land and improvements combined in 2026 | City release |
| 2025-10-31 | Proposed budget: single rate of 6.0439 mills on $6.02B (August base), $33,092,721.82 budgeted | proposed budget |
| 2025-11-04 | Cognetti re-elected; Smurl loses his council seat; King did not run | Fox56; press |
| 2025-11-14 | Judge Gibbons denies the County's request to delay the reassessment; roll certified at $5,857,828,107 | WVIA 2025-11-14; FOC 107 |
| 2025-11-25 | A draft 2026 millage ordinance on the agenda is withdrawn | `sources/scranton_ord_millage2026_2025-11-25_draft_pulled.pdf` |
| 2025-12-02 | FOC 107 introduced at 6.35095037123 mills | minutes |
| 2025-12-09 | Budget adopted; Fidelity building purchase passes 3-2 | minutes |
| 2025-12-16 | Millage adopted 5-0 | minutes |

The final budget re-solved the rate to the certified base and a lower collection assumption
(89%, down from 91%) while keeping the budgeted revenue at exactly $33,092,721.82. That
re-solve is the likeliest reason the 11-25 draft was pulled, though no document says so. The
proposed budget also assumed the Commonwealth Health hospitals would leave the tax rolls on
their sale to a nonprofit. That would cut taxable value by about $186.6M and revenue by about
$1.1M, and the final budget drops the paragraph. Tenor Health Foundation took over Moses Taylor
and Regional hospitals in February 2026.

Two of the five members who voted, Smurl and King, were leaving office. The vote was not
factional: Schuster and McAndrew opposed the mayor on the Fidelity purchase a week earlier and
still voted for the rate.

## 3. What bears on reasons nobody stated

### 3.1 The repeal and the reassessment arrived together

On the 1968-base roll, land paid most of the City tax. Old-roll figures, from the 2025 County
roll:

- the taxable roll reproduces the 2025 levy at
  <!--AUTOGEN:LEVY_CHECK-->$37,197,367 (-0.01% against the ordinance)<!--/AUTOGEN:LEVY_CHECK-->;
- land was <!--AUTOGEN:LAND_SHARE_AV-->24%<!--/AUTOGEN:LAND_SHARE_AV--> of taxable value and
  paid <!--AUTOGEN:LAND_SHARE_TAX-->59%<!--/AUTOGEN:LAND_SHARE_TAX--> of the levy.

The reassessment reset every parcel's land and building values to market. With a split rate,
the City would have had to pick a new land/building ratio and solve two revenue-neutral rates.
The result would have moved bills by parcel land share, separately from the reassessment's own
shifts. The County has not published the land share of the new roll, so the size of that second
shift is unknown. It needs the 2026 County extract (see `rtk_tracker.csv`).

Pittsburgh is the close precedent. Its 2001 rescission followed a reassessment that raised land
values 81% against 43% for buildings, during a mayoral race. Hughes (2006, Lincoln Institute,
`sources/why_repeal/hughes_2006_why_so_little_georgism_lincoln_wp.pdf`) notes that the
reassessment changed several things at once and blurred which caused what. A single rate lets
any shift from land to buildings read as the County's reassessment rather than a City choice.

Land values were also the least trusted part of the new roll. The County's new majority sought a
one-year delay over notice defects and alleged vendor errors (WVIA, 2025-11-10 to 11-14), and
about 4,200 formal appeals were filed. A land rate 4.7 times the building rate multiplies any
land-valuation error in the bill by the same factor.

  found.

### 3.4 Donors

- **Fidelity Bank board.** Board members gave to the mayor's campaign in April 2025. The Free
  Beacon put it at $1,500 each from all nine; the Times-Tribune, citing Politico, at $14,600
  combined. One of them, board secretary John T. Cognetti, is a commercial real estate broker
  and the mayor's uncle by marriage. Councilman McAndrew raised the donations on 2025-12-09 in
  voting against the City's purchase of Fidelity's former building next to City Hall. That
  dispute was about the purchase; no source links it to the tax rate. No board member appears
  as a Scranton owner under their own name on the roll.
- **Everyone else.** Itemized reports for the mayor's committee (Friends of Paige Cognetti) and
  the council candidates are filed with the County and not posted online, so no other donors
  could be checked.

### 3.5 Lobbying and public pressure

No group was found publicly urging repeal: not the Chamber, landlords, downtown owners, parking
operators or developers, in searches of 2020-2025 coverage. One fact cuts against a
pro-parking motive. In 2024 the administration sued its own Zoning Board to block a private
surface lot at 301 Penn Ave, owned by Linden Taylor LLC.

## 4. Hypotheses

Each is consistent with the record; none is stated in it. Listed from best to least supported.

1. **Keep the reassessment year legible.** One rate makes every bill change the County's
   reassessment rather than a City choice of land ratio, at a time when property taxes were
   a live issue in the mayor's congressional race. *Would confirm:* internal emails or a memo
   modeling bills under both options (Request 4). *Would refute:* a pre-2025 plan to drop the
   split independent of the reassessment.
2. **Distrust of the new land values.** A 4.7x land rate magnifies land-valuation errors, and
   land values were contested. *Would confirm:* staff discussion of land appeals or Tyler's
   land model (Request 4).
3. **Administrative simplicity, as stated.** Solving two revenue-neutral rates under the
   anti-windfall rule, on a base certified two weeks before adoption, is more work and more
   risk. This needs no hidden motive. *Would confirm:* the absence of any distributional
   analysis in the City's files.
4. **Serving land-heavy owners or donors.** The largest gains go to land-heavy holders, but no
   record ties any of them to the decision. *Would confirm:* donations from
   the large gainers (Request 5), or an official's interest in one (Request 3).
5. **Officials' own bills.** The officials' matched parcels are ordinary homes plus Smurl's
   rentals. The old-roll changes are hundreds of dollars in both directions, which is the
   weakest of these.
